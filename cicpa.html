Understanding POA

What is a Power of Attorney
and When Do You Need One?

A power of attorney (POA) is a legal document in which one person, the principal, authorises another person, the attorney, to act on their behalf. In the UAE it is normally signed before a notary public, and it can be general or limited to a specific purpose.

People use a POA when they cannot be present in person: selling or buying property, handling bank accounts, transferring a vehicle, managing a company, collecting documents, or appointing a lawyer for a case. Because the attorney can legally act in your name, the wording of the document matters. Vague or overly broad authority is one of the most common risks, and a clear, purpose-based POA is usually safer.

Requirements can differ by notary and by the authority that will accept the document, so Tamkin Express confirms what applies to your case first. We also support the paperwork around it through our PRO services and employment visa services. This page is general information, not legal advice. For disputes or complex cases, speak to a licensed lawyer.

Documents Required

Power of Attorney Documents —
Typical Checklist

The documents below are the typical requirements for a power of attorney in the UAE. Individual notaries and receiving authorities can ask for additional items, so we confirm the exact list for your case during the consultation.

For a company POA, the signatory needs authority to sign for the company, and the trade licence is usually required. Need a licence first? See mainland trade license & LLC registration, or Ministry of Economy services for renewals and amendments.

If the POA will be used outside the UAE, or a POA signed abroad will be used here, extra attestation and translation steps usually apply. Our certificate attestation services cover these, and you can also read about our notary & court services.

Before signing, make sure the Emirates ID or passport is valid and the powers are written clearly. A short, specific list of authorised acts avoids rejection and misuse.

Principal's IDValid Emirates ID or passport of the person granting the POA.
Attorney's DetailsName, nationality and identification details of the person receiving authority.
Scope of PowersA clear list of the acts the attorney may perform and, if needed, the validity period.
Property or Vehicle DetailsTitle deed, vehicle registration or account reference, if the POA relates to them.
Company DocumentsTrade licence and signatory authority for company POAs.
