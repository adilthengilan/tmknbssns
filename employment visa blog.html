What is an Employment Visa in UAE?

An employment visa in UAE — also known as a work residence visa — is the official authorisation that allows a foreign national to legally live and work in the United Arab Emirates. It is issued by the UAE government and is tied to a specific employer and job role.

The UAE employment visa is not a single document. It is a combination of two separate approvals that work together:

  • Work Permit (MOHRE) — Issued by the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation. This authorises the employee to legally work for a specific employer in UAE mainland.
  • Residence Visa (ICA) — Issued by the Federal Authority for Identity and Citizenship. This authorises the employee to legally reside in UAE for the duration of employment.

Both approvals are mandatory for every employee working in a UAE mainland company. Free zone employees follow a slightly different process through their respective free zone authority.

Who Needs an Employment Visa in UAE?

Every foreign national seeking employment in UAE mainland must have a valid employment visa before they can legally begin work. This applies to:

  • New employees being hired from outside UAE
  • Employees already in UAE on a tourist or visit visa being hired by a UAE company
  • Employees transferring from one UAE company to another
  • Employees renewing their existing employment visa upon expiry

UAE nationals are exempt from the employment visa requirement. GCC nationals have a simplified process. All other nationalities must follow the standard UAE employment visa process.

Employment Visa UAE — Step by Step Process

The UAE employment visa process consists of six main steps. Understanding each step helps you prepare the right documents and avoid delays.

1
Work Permit Application — MOHRE The employer submits a work permit application through the MOHRE (Wages Protection System) portal. This requires the employee's passport, educational certificates, and a signed labour contract. Approval typically takes 24–48 hours.
2
Entry Permit Issuance Once the work permit is approved, an entry permit is issued by ICA. This allows the employee to enter UAE for the purpose of employment if they are outside the country. Entry permits are valid for 60 days.
3
Status Change (if already in UAE) If the employee is already in UAE on a tourist or visit visa, a status change application is submitted to convert their status to a residence visa. This avoids the need to exit and re-enter the country.
4
Medical Fitness Test All employees must undergo a medical fitness test at a UAE government-approved health centre. The test screens for communicable diseases. Results are typically available within 24–48 hours.
5
Emirates ID Registration Biometric registration and Emirates ID application is submitted through the Federal Authority for Identity and Citizenship. The Emirates ID serves as the official UAE identification document for the employee.
6
Residence Visa Stamping The final residence visa is stamped in the employee's passport — completing the employment visa process. The visa is valid for 2 years and must be renewed before expiry.

Documents Required for Employment Visa in UAE

Preparing complete and accurate documents is the most important step in ensuring your UAE employment visa application is approved without delays or rejections. Here is the complete list of documents required:

Employee Documents

  • Original passport — valid for a minimum of 6 months from the date of application
  • Passport-size photograph — white background, recent
  • Educational certificates — attested by the relevant UAE authority (MOFA, Ministry of Education)
  • Previous UAE visa copy — if the employee has been in UAE before
  • Medical fitness test results — from a UAE-approved health centre

Employer Documents

  • Valid UAE trade licence — copy of the employer's current trade licence
  • Signed labour contract — MOHRE-approved employment contract
  • Establishment card — MOHRE establishment card of the company
  • Sponsor's Emirates ID copy — the authorised signatory's Emirates ID

Employment Visa Renewal in UAE

UAE employment visas are valid for 2 years and must be renewed before expiry to avoid overstay fines and legal complications. The renewal process follows similar steps to the initial application, including a new medical fitness test and updated documents.

Key renewal deadlines:

  • Renewal application should be submitted at least 30 days before expiry
  • Overstay fines begin at AED 200 per day after the visa expiry date
  • Employees must not leave UAE on an expired visa

Employment Visa Cancellation in UAE

When an employment relationship ends, the employment visa must be officially cancelled through MOHRE and ICA. The cancellation process includes:

  • Cancellation of the work permit through MOHRE
  • Cancellation of the residence visa through ICA
  • Settlement of any outstanding dues as per the labour contract
  • Employee must leave UAE or obtain a new visa within the grace period (30 days)

Failure to cancel the visa properly can result in fines for the employer and legal complications for the employee. Tamkin Express handles the complete visa cancellation process in compliance with UAE Labour Law.

Family Visa Sponsorship on Employment Visa

Employees holding a UAE employment visa can sponsor their family members — spouse, children, and parents — for UAE residence visas, subject to minimum salary requirements.

  • Spouse sponsorship: Minimum salary of AED 4,000/month (or AED 3,000 + accommodation)
  • Children sponsorship: No minimum salary requirement (boys up to age 18, girls until marriage)
  • Parents sponsorship: Minimum salary of AED 20,000/month

Common Reasons for Employment Visa Rejection in UAE

Understanding why employment visas are rejected helps you avoid the same mistakes. The most common rejection reasons include:

  • Unattested educational certificates — the most common reason for rejection
  • Passport with less than 6 months validity
  • Incorrect or incomplete labour contract
  • Medical fitness test failure — communicable disease detected
  • Employer quota exceeded — company has no available visa quota
  • Expired establishment card — employer's MOHRE registration has lapsed
  • Nationality restrictions — certain nationalities require additional approvals
