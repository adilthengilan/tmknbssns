What Is a UAE Employment Visa in Dubai?

Legal requirements every Dubai employer must understand — 2025

A UAE employment visa is a government-issued residency permit that authorises a foreign national to legally live and work in the UAE under employer sponsorship. In Dubai, the employer is the legal visa sponsor — responsible for initiating, maintaining, and cancelling the visa when employment ends.

Under UAE labour law, every foreign national employed by a Dubai-based company must hold a valid employment visa and MOHRE work permit. There are no exceptions. This applies to all mainland companies licensed by DET (Dubai Economy and Tourism) and all free zone companies — DMCC, JAFZA, DIFC, DIC, Dubai Media City, Dubai Internet City, and others.

The employment visa process in Dubai involves five government authorities: MOHRE (Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation), GDRFA / ICA (Federal Authority for Identity and Citizenship), and EIDA (Emirates Identity Authority). TamkinExpress manages every interaction with all authorities on behalf of the employer — no government portal visits required from your HR team.

How long does employment visa processing take in Dubai?
The complete Dubai employment visa process — from MOHRE work permit to residency visa stamping — typically takes 2 to 4 weeks when all documents are correctly submitted. The five stages: MOHRE work permit (3–5 working days), GDRFA entry permit (2–5 working days), medical fitness test (1–3 working days), Emirates ID biometrics (2–7 working days), and residency visa stamping (3–7 working days). TamkinExpress manages every stage with daily follow-up to ensure the fastest possible processing.
What documents are required for a UAE employment visa in Dubai?
Employer documents required: valid Dubai trade licence (DET for mainland, or specific free zone licence), establishment card (Tawjeeh card), and signed employment contract. Employee documents required: valid passport with at least 6 months remaining validity, passport-size photograph, and educational certificates for professional visa categories. TamkinExpress provides a complete, role-specific document checklist after a free consultation.
What are the fines for expired employment visas in Dubai?
An expired employment visa in Dubai results in AED 25 per day in overstay fines, accumulating from the day of expiry until the visa is renewed, transferred, or the employee exits the UAE. The employer is jointly liable for all overstay fines — they must be settled before a new or renewed visa can be processed. Employing a worker without a valid MOHRE work permit carries penalties exceeding AED 50,000 under UAE labour law. TamkinExpress maintains a proactive compliance calendar for every client.

What is inside country vs outside country employment visa processing in Dubai?
Inside country processing (change of status) allows the employee to remain in the UAE throughout — faster and less disruptive for employees already in Dubai on a valid visa. Not all visa statuses qualify. Outside country processing requires the employee to exit the UAE and re-enter on the employment entry permit — always available for all nationalities and all visa categories. The entry permit is valid for 60 days from issue. TamkinExpress assesses each employee's current visa status and recommends the most suitable and cost-effective route.

What is the difference between Dubai mainland and Dubai free zone employment visa?
Dubai mainland employment visas are issued under DET-licensed company sponsorship, processed through MOHRE and GDRFA. Dubai free zone employment visas — for companies in DMCC, JAFZA, DIFC, DIC, and other free zones — are processed through the specific free zone authority alongside MOHRE and GDRFA. Each free zone has distinct documentation requirements and processing portals. TamkinExpress handles both mainland and all Dubai free zone employment visas.
How much does an employment visa cost in Dubai?
Dubai employment visa government fees vary based on the visa category, company type (mainland or free zone), and processing route (inside or outside country). Additional costs include MOHRE work permit fee, GDRFA entry permit fee, medical fitness test fee, and Emirates ID application fee. Who is legally responsible for an employment visa in Dubai?
In Dubai, the employer is the visa sponsor and bears full legal and financial responsibility for the employee's residency status throughout their employment. This includes initiating the visa, maintaining compliance, paying any overstay fines, and — when employment ends — cancelling the visa promptly. Failure to cancel visas for departed employees blocks quota slots and triggers compliance penalties from MOHRE.
What is the validity of a UAE employment visa in Dubai?
A standard UAE employment visa in Dubai is valid for 2 years and is digitally linked to the Emirates ID. The employment entry permit (issued before the employee enters the UAE) is valid for 60 days from the date of issue — the employee must complete the visa stamping process within this window.
