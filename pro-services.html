Understanding PRO Services

What are PRO Services
in the UAE?

PRO stands for Public Relations Officer. In the UAE business context, a PRO is the official representative of a company who handles all government-related procedures on behalf of the business — submitting documents, obtaining approvals, renewing licences, and managing all official correspondence with UAE government authorities.

For most businesses in the UAE, maintaining an in-house PRO team is expensive and inefficient. Government procedures require deep knowledge of specific portals, documentation requirements, and authority workflows that change frequently. A single error — a missing document, an incorrect form, or a missed deadline — can result in visa bans, permit freezes, or fines that cost far more than the PRO service itself.

Outsourcing PRO services to a specialist company like Tamkin Express gives businesses access to a dedicated team of government documentation experts — without the overhead of an in-house hire. Our team handles everything from day-to-day visa renewals to complex multi-ministry approvals, ensuring your business is always compliant. Investors and key talent can also be supported with the UAE Golden Visa.

The Cost of
Unmanaged PRO

Many businesses underestimate the financial impact of unmanaged government documentation. A single lapsed visa or expired trade licence can trigger a cascade of penalties that far exceed the cost of professional PRO services. Without proactive management, businesses routinely face overstay fines, permit freezes, and regulatory violations.

The most common issues include expired employment visas resulting in daily overstay fines, trade licences lapsing and suspending all company activities, MOHRE permit freezes preventing new hiring, and document rejections causing costly resubmission delays.

Tamkin Express maintains a compliance calendar for every client — tracking every expiry date, renewal window, and regulatory deadline so violations are caught early. Our proactive approach means you are never caught off guard by a government deadline.
