What Are Notary & Court Services in Dubai?

In the UAE, notarized documents are a legal requirement for hundreds of personal, business, and property transactions.

Notary and court services in Dubai refer to the official authentication, certification, and legal attestation of documents through the UAE Ministry of Justice, Dubai Court Notary Public offices, or federal notary authorities. These services are legally required before a document is recognized by UAE government bodies, banks, courts, real estate registries, or international embassies.

Common documents that require notarization in Dubai include Power of Attorney (POA), Memorandum of Association (MOA), tenancy contracts, sale agreements, affidavits, personal status declarations, and educational certificates. Without proper notarization, these documents carry no legal standing in the UAE.

Notarization and attestation are two distinct legal processes in the UAE, and both are often required depending on how the document will be used. Notarization is performed by a UAE Notary Public and verifies the authenticity of a document's content, the identity of the signing parties, and the legality of the transaction — it is required for documents used within the UAE for legal transactions such as property sales, Power of Attorney, and business agreements.

Attestation is an additional process where a government authority — such as the UAE Ministry of Justice, Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MOFA), or a foreign embassy — officially endorses the notarized document so it is legally recognized in another country or by an international body. In simple terms: notarization is for local UAE legal use, attestation is for international recognition. Many documents require both processes. TamkinExpress provides both notarization and attestation services in Dubai, and can advise which process your specific document requires.

The notarization process officially confirms the identity of the person granting the POA, verifies the document was signed willingly and without coercion, and registers the POA with the UAE Ministry of Justice.

For a UAE-notarized POA to be used outside the UAE — for example, to authorize someone to manage assets or sign contracts abroad — the document must additionally be attested by MOFA and the relevant foreign embassy.
