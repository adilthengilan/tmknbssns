Understanding MOHRE

What is the Ministry of Labour
(MOHRE) in the UAE?

The Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MOHRE) — previously known as the Ministry of Labour — is the UAE federal authority responsible for regulating the private sector labour market. Every private sector employer operating in the UAE falls under its jurisdiction. MOHRE governs how businesses hire, manage, and release employees, ensuring the rights of both workers and companies are protected under UAE Federal Labour Law.

For any business in the UAE, dealing with MOHRE is not a one-time event. It is an ongoing compliance requirement that touches every stage of the employment lifecycle — from the moment a company receives its hiring quota to the day an employee's final clearance is processed. Missing a step, submitting incorrect documents, or misclassifying a worker's permit category can result in financial penalties, permit freezes, or bans that directly affect your ability to hire.

This is why businesses across Abu Dhabi and Dubai choose to work with experienced MOHRE PRO service providers like Tamkin Express — to ensure every application, renewal, and cancellation is handled correctly, on time, and without unnecessary delays.

Permit Types

UAE Work Permit Categories
Explained

One of the most common mistakes UAE employers make is applying for the wrong work permit category. MOHRE classifies work permits based on the employee's education level, skill type, and the nature of employment. Choosing the wrong category leads to application rejections, additional costs, and processing delays that hold up your hire.

Labour Contracts

UAE Labour Contract Requirements
Under the 2022 Labour Law

The UAE Labour Law was significantly reformed in 2022 under Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021. One of the most important changes was the abolition of unlimited employment contracts. All new employment contracts in the UAE private sector must now be fixed-term contracts, with a maximum duration of three years. Contracts can be renewed upon mutual agreement.

Every MOHRE-registered labour contract must include the employee's job title, basic salary, allowances, working hours, annual leave entitlement, notice period, and end-of-service gratuity terms. Contracts that are missing mandatory clauses can be challenged by employees and may not be enforceable in a labour dispute.

At Tamkin Express, we prepare all labour contracts in accordance with current UAE Labour Law requirements, register them with MOHRE, and provide both employer and employee with a compliant copy. We also calculate end-of-service gratuity entitlements to ensure accurate obligations are reflected in the contract from day one.

Fixed-Term OnlyAll UAE private sector contracts must be fixed-term, maximum 3 years, renewable by agreement.
Mandatory ClausesJob title, salary, allowances, working hours, leave, notice period — all must be explicitly stated.
MOHRE RegistrationContracts must be registered through the MOHRE portal before the employee commences work legally.
End-of-Service GratuityEmployees are entitled to gratuity after one year of service, calculated on the basic salary as per Labour Law.
Probation LimitsProbation periods cannot exceed six months and must be clearly stated in the registered contract.

The Cost of
Non-Compliance

UAE employers who fail to meet their MOHRE obligations face penalties that go beyond simple fines. A permit freeze — which MOHRE issues to non-compliant companies — stops all new work permit applications until the violation is resolved. For a growing business, this can mean being unable to hire for weeks or months.

The most common violations include expired work permits, unregistered labour contracts, failure to meet Emiratisation targets, and late salary payments flagged through the Wage Protection System (WPS). Each carries its own penalty structure, and in serious cases, MOHRE can initiate legal proceedings against the company's authorised signatory.

Working with Tamkin Express means having a dedicated team monitoring your compliance calendar — permit expiry dates, renewal windows, and Emiratisation quota deadlines — so violations never catch you off guard.
